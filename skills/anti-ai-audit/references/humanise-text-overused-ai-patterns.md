# Overused AI Patterns Reference

This file is the language-layer blacklist for the anti-AI audit: it catches copy that sounds generated, over-optimised, or coded to sound like a pitch rather than a human statement.

## Structural patterns to reject

- Elliptical setups that try to create intrigue through a built-in question
- Contrarian reveal hooks that frame the writer as exposing a hidden truth
- Big-contrast constructions that rely on “it’s not X, it’s Y” phrasing
- Conceptual reframes that sound polished but are just forced opposites
- Philosophical reductions that package a complex idea into a slogan

## Phrase and vocabulary checks

The text should avoid overused transition words, business jargon, and marketing fluff that flatten plain language.

Examples of what to reject include:
- moreover, furthermore, therefore, additionally, consequently, despite, notably
- leverage, synergy, streamline, optimize, empower, seamless, frictionless, turnkey, thought leadership, paradigm shift
- cutting-edge, next-generation, best-in-class, user-centric, data-driven, mission-critical, game-changer

## Expression patterns that read as AI-generated

- “As a result”
- “As well as”
- “In order to”
- “Due to”
- “Given that”
- “At the end of the day”

Prefer direct, plain language instead.

## Hard rule

Avoid em-dashes. Rewrite the line so it reads naturally without forcing a rhetorical flourish.

## Humanization goal

The target is not polished-sounding prose. It is clear, grounded, natural language that feels written by a person rather than generated to sound smart.
